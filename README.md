# Social-Media-Dopamine-Addiction-Vs-Productivity-Dataset
## 📑 Introduction & Dataset Facts
* **Participant Base:** Analyzes survey responses from **300 global participants** across **39 unique countries**.
* **Screen Time Engagement:** Captures a high average daily screen time of **7.83 hours** per user, with peak addiction scores reaching up to **9.90 hours**.
* **Demographic Coverage:** Covers 7 distinct employment groups (led by **62.67% full-time employees** and **24% students**) across various age brackets.
* **Core Metrics Tracked:** Evaluates behavioral indicators including **restlessness scores (avg. 4.14)**, **anxiety levels when phone is unavailable (avg. 4.30)**, task-switching frequency, and sleep quality scores.

## ❓ Problem Statement
Excessive social media consumption creates continuous dopamine rush cycles, leading to compulsive phone checking and fragmented focus during work or study hours. The objective of this project is to analyze how daily screen time and primary platform usage impact deep work duration, task-switching habits, and overall mental well-being, providing data-backed thresholds to mitigate productivity loss.

## 💡 Key Business Recommendations
* **Set Screen Time Limits at 6 Hours:** Keep daily non-work screen usage below 6 hours, as users spending **over 7.83 hours** show a significant drop in focus and higher restlessness (avg. 4.14).
* **Implement 90-Minute Focus Blocks:** Schedule 90-minute uninterrupted deep-work sprints to reduce frequent task-switching, which directly degrades sleep and work quality scores.
* **Address High-Trigger Platforms:** Limit access to short-form visual platforms (like Instagram with **95 users** and Facebook with **89 users**) during core hours to minimize dopamine rushes and withdrawal anxiety (avg. 4.30).
* **Promote Evening Digital Wellness:** Encourage turning off notifications 1 to 2 hours before sleep to improve sleep quality tiers, which directly correlated with a **work quality rating boost from 2.40 to 9.58**
